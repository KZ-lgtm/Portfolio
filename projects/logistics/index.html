Overview

Rebuilding a real warehouse cell, then making it safe

The starting point was ABB Robotics' public "Logistics" video of a Cainiao warehouse, where robots work around an automated cylinder shelf. The video shows the idea but not the numbers, and its pickers and sorters stand next to open walkways.

I rebuilt the cell with robots from the ABB library, using real kinematics and joint limits, and ran it as an order-fulfilment system: rolling waves of orders, dynamic bin slotting, a 3D image before every pick, bar-code checks and an exception route for no-reads.

Running the model for hours exposed problems the video could not: a wrist singularity in the shelf robot, pickers reaching over their own bases, bins queuing at the wrong stop. Each one was fixed in the layout and recorded in the ISO 12100 risk assessment.

Products, orders, speeds and stopping times are assumptions. This is a concept study, not ABB's installation.

Process sequence

One order, from shelf to packing

  1. Plan the wave

    The WMS gives each put-wall lane an order and adds up open lines per SKU.

  2. Fetch the bin

    R1 takes the bin from the shelf to the east port. Returning bins get the nearest free slot.

  3. Pick with 3D vision

    At stop P1 or P2 a 3D image is taken, then R2 or R3 places the carton into a free gap on the item belt.

  4. Scan

    A bar-code arch reads every carton. No-reads go to an exception tote outside the fence.

  5. Sort

    S1 or S2 puts the carton into the order case on its lane.

  6. Pack and restock

    Finished cases run out to the packer. Empty bins leave by the west port for restock.

Design decisions

What the simulation and risk assessment changed

Items rated High after the first safeguarding pass, what the problem was, and how the design was changed. IDs refer to the hazard list in the assessment.

H2Guarding

One guarded zone instead of open robots

Problem

In the video the pickers and sorters stand next to open walkways with no visible guarding. Only the shelf encloses the shelf robot.

Resolution

The whole system is one fenced zone. Material only crosses at the restock tunnel, the 8 put-wall lanes and a covered exception chute. Operators work outside.

9 laser scanners, 100 % coverage incl. the ring interior
H1Tooling

An L-fork to escape a wrist singularity

Problem

The first fork, mounted on a sideways flange, put the R1 wrist into the A5 = 0 singularity at mid levels. A4 and A6 turned about a full revolution for every bin move.

Resolution

L-fork under a downward flange, bin centre 0.55 m ahead of the TCP. All 8 levels in reach with a calm wrist.

A6 travel 18 000° per hour → ±23°
H1Shelf

Room for the wrist at the top level

Problem

With a 0.45 m fork offset the wrist reached the shelf face at the top level and rose into the top ring beam at 3.35 m.

Resolution

Fork offset increased to 0.55 m and the top ring raised to 3.75 m, keeping the arm inside the shelf face outside the served column.

0 shelf contacts in 4 × 1 h
H2Paths

Pickers that stop reaching over themselves

Problem

R2 and R3 stand between the bin stop and the item belt, so straight moves passed over their own bases.

Resolution

Bases turned to face west and every bin-to-belt move swings round the west side at r ≥ 0.6 m.

94 reach failures in 120 s → 0
H7Safeguards

Hand-detection curtains at the put wall

Problem

A whole-body curtain at the put-wall tunnel mouths needs 1250 mm of safety distance. Only 970 mm are available to the sorter cup.

Resolution

Tunnel mouths cut to 0.37 × 0.30 m so no one can climb in, guarded by 30 mm hand-detection curtains.

Required 528 mm ≤ 970 mm available
H8Flow

Choosing the bin stop at the right moment

Problem

Picking the stop when a bin arrived at the decision point sent most bins to P1 while P2 was still clearing. R3 sat idle and bins queued behind P1.

Resolution

The stop is chosen when the bin can actually leave the decision point: P2 unless a bin is already at or heading to it.

R3 3 % busy → R2 / R3 43 / 40 %
H2Electrical

Moving a controller to shorten its cables

Problem

With RC2 south of the cell, the R2 / R3 manipulator cables were over 30 m and its service area sat in the door swing. The north trunk had a 3.9 m unsupported span.

Resolution

RC2 moved north beside the shelf passage, a ladder tray added over the put-wall tunnels, junction boxes put on the trunk.

Manipulator cables 31.9 m → ≤ 15.4 m

Result: no residual High risk. Four hazards stay Medium (catastrophic severity, remote probability), which is inherent to a shelf robot and a 3.75 m storage ring and tolerable with SF1–SF7 implemented and the shelf designed and inspected to EN 15512 / EN 15635. Everything else is Low or Negligible.
