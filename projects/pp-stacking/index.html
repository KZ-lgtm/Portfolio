Overview

A line that cannot stop, and sheets that must be flipped

Processes 4 to 6 of a PP flooring line: a 2-roller embosser and a flying cutter produce sheets 1000 mm wide and 1000, 1300 or 1500 mm long. The extrusion upstream runs continuously, so every stop in the stacking cell pushes sheets back against the cutter.

The sheets have to be stacked decor face to decor face, so every second one is turned over. The cell does that with the robot itself: a fork gripper bolted directly to the A6 flange runs under the sheet on the pick table, clamps it, and axis 6 rolls it 180° on the way to the pallet.

I built the cell as an interactive 3D model with real robot kinematics, used it to measure cycle times and clearances, and wrote the risk assessment against the same layout. Each finding changed the model, and the model is what the assessment describes.

Process sequence

One sheet, start to pallet

  1. Emboss and cut

    2-roller embossing (4), traction and flying cutter (5) deliver 6 sheets/min.

  2. Buffer and infeed

    Run-out and infeed buffer belts feed the sheet into the open fork on the pick table.

  3. Clamp and flip

    The robot clamps and lifts. Axis 6 flips every second sheet 180°, face to face.

  4. Stack

    Set down 10 mm above the stack at station 3. The stripper holds the sheet while the tines slide out.

  5. Index and ship

    Full pallet moves to the 4–6 buffer, the stacker feeds an empty one, AGV or forklift ships from 6.

Design decisions

What the risk assessment changed

Every item rated High after the first safeguarding pass, what the problem was, and how the design was changed to remove it. IDs refer to the hazard list in the assessment.

H1Gripper

Let the robot do the flipping

Problem

The first gripper carried its own flip unit, a servo and gearbox under the flange: about 38 kg with the centre of gravity 0.55 m out. Too heavy for the IRB 4600-40 wrist with a sheet on it.

Resolution

Fork bolted straight to the A6 flange, flange facing along the tines, so axis 6 is the flip axis. No flip drive, shorter lever. Wrist checked over the full cycle.

38 kg → 22 kg
H5Guarding

Move the light curtains out to a pallet tunnel

Problem

Curtains at the station 3 fence opening would stand 0.38 m from the fork. ISO 13855 needs 1730 mm at a 0.5 s stopping time, so a person could reach the fork through the muted opening.

Resolution

Stations 2–4 enclosed in a fenced tunnel with the curtains at its ends. Enough distance even if the real stopping time turns out longer.

2480 mm available ≥ 1890 mm at 0.6 s
H2Motion

A fixed, interlocked flip point

Problem

A 1.40 m fork rolling 180° sweeps a large disc. Flipped tangentially it hits the forearm; flipped over the table or pallet it hits equipment.

Resolution

Flip only at one point over free floor, fork radial within ±15°, enabled by a SafeMove zone and clamp-closed sensors (SF7).

0 flips outside the zone in a 1 h run
H10Throughput

Buffers for a line that cannot stop

Problem

A sequential pallet change took 18 s, long enough to push sheets to manual off-load even in normal running. Any door opening or E-stop meant sheets piling against the cutter.

Resolution

Run-out and infeed buffer belts, a manual off-load exit outside the hood, and the empty pallet following the full one.

Pallet change 18 s → 7–9 s · 0 sheets off-loaded in 1 h
H1Paths

Clearances on fast blended paths

Problem

Blending the return and approach paths to reach 6/min brought the fork backbone 0.48 m from the robot axis and the tool 22–52 mm over the pick table.

Resolution

Return via point turned 15°, table approach raised 0.26 m above the flip axis, tines withdrawn past the pallet edge.

All sweeps ≥ 0.11 m over equipment
H17Electrical

A routing bug found by the cable check

Problem

The route from the robot controller to the main panel was missing although both sat on tray T1. The tray graph split a node at y = 0 into "0.00" and "−0.00".

Resolution

Node keys normalised in the routing engine, then all 8 stations re-verified. JB1 and JB2 moved out of the line keep-out next to a cable carrier.

8 / 8 stations, 0 issues

Result: no residual High risk. Five hazards stay Medium (catastrophic severity, remote probability), which is inherent to a robot cell and tolerable with safety functions SF1–SF8 implemented and validated. Everything else is Low or Negligible.
